## The short answer: AI search also has to find your site first

When one of your customers asks ChatGPT, "Can you recommend a boutique hotel in Safranbolu that feels like an old mansion and has a good breakfast?", the system usually does not make up the answer from its own memory. It searches the web, reads the pages it finds and builds its answer on them. For your hotel to be on that list, the search first has to be able to find your site, and then understand the information on your page.

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the general name for the work done to make this process easier. It may sound like a new discipline, yet most of the work overlaps with well-executed SEO. In this post we have tried to separate the steps that actually help you appear in AI search from the claims that are overstated.

## What is GEO, and where did the idea come from?

The term spread through the paper "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", written by researchers from Princeton and other universities. The paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and colleagues was published at the KDD 2024 conference ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)). The researchers measured that changes to content could increase visibility in AI answers by up to 40 per cent. The best results came from citing sources, adding quotations and adding statistics; keyword stuffing did not work. The paper also stresses that this effect varies by domain. The 40 per cent is an upper limit and should not be treated as an average that can be promised to every site.

## Where do these systems get their information?

Each AI tool reaches the web by its own route. Here is the summary a small business needs:

| System | Where does it find information? | What you need to do |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews and AI Mode | Google's own search index | Get your pages indexed by Google |
| ChatGPT search | OpenAI's OAI-SearchBot crawler | Allow OAI-SearchBot in robots.txt |
| Perplexity | The PerplexityBot crawler | Allow PerplexityBot in robots.txt |
| Microsoft Copilot | The Bing index | Register with Bing Webmaster Tools |
| Local searches | Google Business Profile and other sources on the web | Keep your profile complete and up to date |

**Google** has been clear on this. According to the "AI features and your website" document on Search Central, there are no additional technical requirements for a page to be shown as a link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. It is enough for the page to be indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet. Google also explains that these features run multiple related searches when answering a question ("query fan-out"), which is why they can show a wider range of sources than traditional search ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).

In separate guidance published on 15 May 2026, Google went a step further: because its own AI features are built on its core ranking and quality systems, SEO remains relevant. The guidance highlights original content that cannot be found elsewhere as its most important recommendation ([Google Search Central Blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/05/a-new-resource-for-optimizing), [guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

On the **ChatGPT** side, OpenAI uses a crawler called OAI-SearchBot for search results. According to OpenAI's documentation, sites that block this bot via robots.txt are not shown in ChatGPT's search answers. GPTBot, used for model training, is a separate bot. So if you do not want your content used for training, you can block GPTBot and allow OAI-SearchBot ([OpenAI, Overview of OpenAI Crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)).

**Perplexity** makes a similar distinction. PerplexityBot crawls sites to show and link to them in Perplexity's search results and, according to the company, is not used to train foundation models ([Perplexity Docs](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers)).

**Bing** is the foundation for Copilot and for the AI summaries in Bing. On 10 February 2026, Microsoft added a report called "AI Performance" to Bing Webmaster Tools. The report lists which of your pages are cited as sources in Copilot, in Bing's AI summaries and in some partner integrations. In the same post, Microsoft states that using IndexNow to notify search engines when content is added or updated helps AI answers use the current version of a page ([Bing Webmaster Blog](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview/)).

## Google Business Profile for local businesses

For searches beginning with "near me", "in Kadıköy" or "in Safranbolu", your Business Profile is decisive. Google says local results are mainly based on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence describes how well known your business is, and draws on information such as the number of websites linking to your business, the reviews you receive and your ratings ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=tr)). This is why it matters that your opening hours, address and phone number are the same on your profile, your website and directories.

## Overstated topics: llms.txt and structured data

On **llms.txt**, Google's position is clear. Its May 2026 guidance states that creating an llms.txt file neither helps nor harms visibility in Google Search. The same guidance says that splitting content into small chunks for AI, or writing separate "AI-friendly" text, is unnecessary ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)).

**Structured data** (schema.org) tells machines what your page is about in a language they understand, and it is worth using. However, from 7 May 2026 Google stopped showing FAQ rich results entirely ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/)). The room for using structured data to gain a visual extra in search results is shrinking; its main benefit is that your content is understood correctly.

## Are clicks falling?

The most widely cited data on this comes from Pew Research Center. According to its study of the browsing data of 900 US adults in March 2025, when an AI summary appeared in the search results, users clicked on a result in 8 per cent of visits. In searches without a summary, the figure was 15 per cent. Clicks on the source links inside the summary occurred in only 1 per cent of visits ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)).

For a small business, this means that being named in an AI answer may not bring visits on its own. When users reach your site, they should find what they are looking for straight away and be able to contact you easily. A [website assistant](/en/ai-assistants/website-assistant) that greets visitors day and night can fill this gap.

## Checklist for small business websites

- Register with Google Search Console, submit your sitemap and check that your important pages are indexed.
- Register with Bing Webmaster Tools; turn on IndexNow if your site supports it.
- Make sure your robots.txt file does not block OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot.
- Complete your Google Business Profile in full; write your address, phone number and hours the same way everywhere.
- At the top of each important page, give a short, clear answer to the question the page addresses.
- Write concrete details such as prices, capacity and location as text; do not leave them only inside images.
- If you use figures, cite the source. This was one of the most effective methods in the GEO paper.
- Add structured data for your organisation, services and local business details.
- Check Bing's AI Performance report from time to time.
